01 · Problem framing

A failed payment is not one problem

When a payment fails, the cause may be temporary bank or network unavailability, authentication friction, insufficient funds, a customer-correctable input issue, risk controls, or a merchant integration problem. Treating all failures alike leads to generic retries, unnecessary customer effort, repeated declines, and potentially higher fraud or processing costs.

Customer / merchant pain

Customers do not know whether to retry, change the payment method, complete authentication, or stop. Merchants lose completed orders and may face support burden.

Business opportunity

Improve legitimate payment completion and reduce avoidable retries and support contacts, while preserving fraud controls, issuer/network rules, and a transparent customer experience.

02 · Diagnose with data

Find the failure modes with the largest recoverable impact

I would start by validating event quality and building a segmented funnel, rather than assuming that every decline can or should be retried.

Analytical cuts

  • Payment method, issuer/acquirer where available, merchant category, device, geography, and time.
  • Failure reason, response-code family, latency, timeout, authentication step, and integration error.
  • First-attempt success, eventual success within a defined window, duplicate attempts, and time to resolution.
  • Merchant-level impact, customer support contacts, refunds/disputes, fraud signals, and processing costs.
  • Separate technical/temporary failures from hard declines and risk-related declines; never blindly retry the latter.
03 · Product proposal

Build a failure-aware recovery layer

Customer-facing recovery

  1. Show a plain-language status that reflects the actual payment state.
  2. Give one appropriate next step: wait, retry once after a safe interval, complete authentication, or choose another payment method.
  3. Preserve checkout context so a retry does not force the customer to re-enter everything.
  4. Protect against duplicate submissions and show a clear final confirmation.
  5. Offer a support path when the state is uncertain or repeated attempts fail.

Platform / operations layer

  1. Normalise failure signals into actionable categories with confidence and source.
  2. Use deterministic rules first for safe retry eligibility, cooldowns, and hard-stop conditions.
  3. Where contractually and technically supported, evaluate approved routing choices using reliability, latency, cost, and risk constraints.
  4. Use an AI assistant to summarise patterns and propose investigations, not to override payment or risk controls.
  5. Expose reason codes, recovery action, final status, and audit trail to merchants.
04 · AI agent with guardrails

Let AI investigate patterns, not make unsafe payment decisions

The AI-assisted component would be a read-only investigation agent for product and operations teams. It could query approved aggregated metrics, detect unusual shifts in failure categories, compare cohorts, summarise evidence, and recommend a human-reviewed investigation.

Allowed actions

  • Query governed aggregate tables and approved metrics.
  • Flag changes against documented baselines.
  • Generate a source-linked summary and candidate hypotheses.
  • Draft an investigation ticket for human approval.

Hard boundaries

  • No access to raw card credentials or unnecessary personal data.
  • No autonomous changes to routing, retry policies, or risk rules.
  • No bypassing issuer/network requirements or merchant controls.
  • Every claim must include data window, sample size, and traceable evidence.
05 · Experiment design

Measure incremental recovery, not just retry volume

ElementPlan
PopulationEligible payment attempts with failure categories approved for a recovery intervention.
ControlExisting payment failure and recovery experience.
TreatmentFailure-aware messaging and category-specific recovery; safe retry rules only where permitted.
Primary metricIncremental successful payments within a defined recovery window among eligible failed attempts.
Secondary metricsTime to successful payment, checkout completion, duplicate attempts, and support-contact rate.
GuardrailsFraud, chargebacks/disputes, duplicate charges, latency, processing cost, customer complaints, and hard-decline retry rate.
MethodRandomised controlled test or a carefully justified phased rollout, with pre-registered eligibility, sample size, duration, and stop conditions.

Baseline success, minimum detectable effect, sample size, and the recovery window must be determined from validated data before launch. No uplift is assumed in this case study.

06 · Prioritisation and delivery

Start narrow, learn quickly, scale safely

Phase 1 · Establish the truth

  • Audit event taxonomy, status transitions, response-code mapping, and duplicate-payment handling.
  • Quantify failure volume, cost, recoverability, and merchant impact by segment.
  • Review customer tickets and interview merchants with high failure burden.

Phase 2 · Test one segment

  • Choose one high-volume, plausibly recoverable failure category.
  • Ship clear status messaging and a controlled recovery flow.
  • Monitor dashboards and alert owners; roll back on guardrail breaches.
07 · Business judgment

Optimise net value, not a single conversion number

The business case should estimate incremental successful payments and retained merchant value, then subtract additional processing costs, infrastructure costs, support load, fraud/dispute exposure, and any negative impact from latency or checkout abandonment. Success means more legitimate payments completed with a better customer experience and sustainable economics, not simply more retries.

Decision rule

Scale only if the experiment demonstrates a credible improvement in successful payment completion, meets agreed latency and cost thresholds, and does not worsen fraud, disputes, duplicate charges, or customer complaints. Iterate if the effect is inconclusive; roll back if safety or reliability guardrails breach.

What this demonstrates

How I would operate as a growth-oriented PM

Use SQL and funnel/cohort analysis to locate the highest-impact problem; understand customer and merchant friction directly; frame a measurable hypothesis; partner with engineering and payment ecosystem stakeholders; use AI to accelerate investigation with governance; and connect product metrics to merchant value, risk, and unit economics.

Prajakta’s product takeaway

Payment recovery should maximise safe completion, not encourage blind retries. Classifying failures by recoverability and giving users a context-aware next step can protect conversion while reducing duplicate attempts and avoidable support. The solution should be validated with a controlled experiment and strict payment-safety guardrails.
