FamPay: Make a failed UPI payment easier to understand and recover

When a payment is pending or money is debited without clear confirmation, the user needs more than a spinner. This concept explores how FamPay could explain the status, prevent accidental repeat payments, and make the next safe step obvious.

1. The problem

A UPI payment can show as pending when the app or a bank has not yet received a final response. The sender may see a debit while the receiver sees no credit. The uncertainty creates three questions: Did my money go? Should I pay again? Who will fix it?

FamPay’s public help content describes payment-blocked scenarios and asks users to share an error screenshot through support. Its older educational article also describes payment-processing states during bank downtime. This is enough to motivate a problem hypothesis, not to claim a verified current failure rate.

Problem statement: Users cannot confidently decide what to do after an uncertain UPI outcome because payment status, expected resolution timing and the safest next action may not be presented together in one clear journey.

2. Who is affected and when?

Primary user

A student or young adult using FamPay for everyday peer-to-peer transfers or merchant payments, who needs the money and confirmation quickly.

High-anxiety moment

The app says pending, the bank balance is lower, and the recipient says the money has not arrived. A second payment could create a duplicate debit.

Jobs to be done

  • Tell me what is known about this transaction in plain language.
  • Help me avoid paying twice while the first payment is unresolved.
  • Tell me when to check again and how to get help if the status does not change.
  • Let me share a safe transaction reference with support without repeating the story.

4. Proposed solution: Payment Status & Recovery

① Clear status

Show Pending, Successful or Failed only when backed by the latest available transaction state. Explain that pending means the final outcome is not yet confirmed.

② Safe next step

For pending payments, advise users not to retry immediately; show a clear refresh/check-again action and a time-aware message based on the bank/network status.

③ One-tap help

Expose transaction ID, amount, time, payee and a support route. Pre-fill details in a ticket while hiding sensitive data from screenshots and analytics.

Interactive low-fidelity concept

This is a clickable concept to demonstrate the UX, not a live connection to FamPay or a payment network.

5. MVP requirements

  1. State accuracy: use the latest backend/network response; never infer success or failure from a timeout alone.
  2. Plain-language copy: explain what is confirmed, what is unknown and what the user should do next.
  3. Safe retry behaviour: avoid a prominent “Pay again” action while the previous transaction is unresolved.
  4. Resolution guidance: show when to check again, the transaction reference, and a support escalation path based on the transaction type and applicable rules.
  5. Accessible support handoff: pre-fill context and make ticket status discoverable.
  6. Privacy and security: do not expose UPI PINs, full account numbers or sensitive identifiers in logs, analytics or support screenshots.

7. What changes, and what improvement should we expect?

The product change is a single Payment Status & Recovery screen that combines an authoritative transaction state, plain-language explanation, safe next action and a visible support route. It replaces the dead end of “pending/failed” with a guided decision.

For users

  1. Pending: explain that the outcome is not final, show last-updated time and reference ID, and discourage an immediate duplicate payment.
  2. Successful: show confirmed completion and the reference details the user can share with the recipient.
  3. Failed / reversal due: explain the next step and show only the applicable, policy-backed reversal guidance.
  4. Still unresolved: offer a one-tap support handoff with transaction ID, timestamp, amount and status prefilled.

For the business

  1. Reduce avoidable repeat payment attempts caused by uncertainty.
  2. Reduce preventable “where is my money?” support contacts, while keeping escalation easy.
  3. Improve trust and repeat usage by making payment outcomes legible.
  4. Give Product and Operations a bank/network-level view of unresolved outcomes.

8. Conclusion: the next step

Recommendation: test the recovery journey before scaling it. Start with one narrow cohort of uncertain-outcome UPI transactions, instrument the current baseline, validate the copy with users, and run a controlled experiment. If the test meets the improvement thresholds without harming safety, expand by bank and transaction type. If it does not, use the failure-state and support data to identify which step needs redesign.

Next 1 · Diagnose

Analyse 4–6 weeks of payment states, repeat attempts, support contacts and reversal outcomes. Segment by bank, payment type, amount and error code.

Next 2 · Prototype + instrument

Run 5–8 usability sessions and implement events for status viewed, help opened, retry attempted, final outcome and support resolution.

Next 3 · Decide with evidence

Run an adequately powered A/B test. Ship only if the primary metric improves and safety guardrails hold; otherwise iterate on the biggest observed drop-off.

Expected business value: fewer avoidable repeat attempts, less preventable support workload and stronger confidence in everyday payments. The actual percentage lift must come from the experiment, not a portfolio guess.

9. Experiment plan

Hypothesis

Showing an accurate status, a safe next action and a visible help route will improve status comprehension and reduce avoidable repeat attempts without delaying legitimate escalation.

Test design

Randomise eligible uncertain-outcome transactions between current experience and the proposed status/recovery page. Stratify by P2P vs merchant payment, bank response code and transaction amount band.

Decision rules

  • Launch only if status accuracy and safety guardrails remain within pre-agreed limits.
  • Compare repeat attempts and support contacts alongside user comprehension, not in isolation.
  • Review results by bank, payment type and app version to avoid hiding concentrated failures.
  • Use a pre-calculated sample size and test duration based on baseline rate and minimum detectable effect.

10. Delivery plan

Week 1 · Discover

Review support tickets, map transaction states, interview users and align with engineering/support on failure codes and dependencies.

Week 2 · Design

Prototype copy and states; test comprehension; write PRD, analytics spec, edge cases and support handoff.

Weeks 3–4 · Validate

Instrument events, QA bank/network edge cases, launch a guarded experiment and review the metrics before rollout.

11. Product trade-offs and risks

  • Clear copy vs false certainty: never promise an exact refund time when the app cannot confirm it.
  • Self-service vs access to help: guide users without burying escalation or making support intentionally hard to reach.
  • Retry prevention vs conversion: avoid duplicate payments while keeping a clear path once a final failed status is confirmed.
  • App control vs bank/network dependencies: the app can improve visibility and coordination, but cannot unilaterally settle or reverse every transaction.

Product takeaway

Trust is part of payment success. The proposed recovery screen makes the solution concrete, while the experiment connects it to business outcomes. The goal is not just to explain a known problem; it is to prove whether clearer status and safer next steps reduce repeat attempts and support load.
