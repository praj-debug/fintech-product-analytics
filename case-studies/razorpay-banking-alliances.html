Banking Alliances: From first contact to a growing commercial partnership.

An end-to-end operating SOP for how I would manage a hunter-led partnerships role for Razorpay Engage: choose the right accounts, identify decision-makers, open conversations, qualify needs, handle rejection, build the commercial case, coordinate internal teams, launch successfully and grow the relationship.

01 · Understand what is being sold

Sell a business outcome, not a catalogue of features.

Razorpay Engage is positioned as a payments-powered growth-marketing suite. Its public portfolio includes Offers Engine, Gift Cards, Loyalty Wallet, Razorpay BillMe, Contextual Marketing Suite and Enterprise Loyalty Programs. The value proposition is to help banks and businesses acquire, retain and reactivate customers through transaction-led incentives and engagement.

Offers & affordability

Co-funded offers, card-linked promotions, checkout and bank-app placements. The bank's outcome: increase card usage, spend and activation while managing incentive cost.

Loyalty & rewards

Wallets, points, vouchers and reward journeys. The bank's outcome: improve engagement, retention, redemption and perceived card value.

Gift cards & media

Gift-card distribution, merchant-funded incentives and contextual campaign inventory. The bank's outcome: new engagement surfaces and partner-funded value for customers.

Product scope and positioning are based on Razorpay's public Engage page. Final packaging, availability and geography must be validated with the product and commercial teams before pitching.

02 · Choose where to hunt

Build a named-account market, then prioritise it.

I would not start by emailing every bank in a spreadsheet. I would create a 40–60-account universe, score it, and work the highest-potential 15–20 accounts first. The exact account count is an initial operating assumption and would be adjusted to territory size and sales-cycle length.

Tier 1 · Strategic issuers

Large retail banks with sizeable debit or credit-card portfolios, active loyalty programmes, digital banking investments, and a clear need to improve activation or spend.

Entry proposition: card-spend growth, loyalty modernisation, co-funded merchant offers, or a new premium-card proposition.

Tier 2 · Challenger banks & fintechs

Digital-first banks, card issuers, NBFCs with card or payments propositions, and fintechs seeking differentiated engagement without building every capability in-house.

Entry proposition: faster campaign launch, rewards infrastructure, gift-card distribution, and measurable engagement.

Tier 3 · Ecosystem multipliers

Card networks, processors, issuer processors, wallet providers, loyalty platforms and large merchant groups that can create distribution or co-selling leverage.

Entry proposition: a repeatable issuer or merchant programme, network-wide campaign distribution, or a joint go-to-market motion.

Geography lens

For APAC, GCC, Africa and Central Europe, I would build a country-by-country map of issuer concentration, card penetration, local scheme rules, data residency, currency, settlement, language and procurement requirements.

Rule: no geography expansion without a local partner thesis and a compliance feasibility check.

04 · How I would open doors

Use account signals to make outbound specific.

Signals I would research

  • New card launches, premium-card refreshes or co-brand announcements.
  • New loyalty programme, points devaluation or rewards-platform migration.
  • Low card activation or spend frequency disclosed in public investor material.
  • Digital-banking app revamp or new customer acquisition push.
  • Merchant campaigns, festive offers or a visible push into affluent customers.
  • Hiring for cards, loyalty, CRM, partnerships or digital product roles.

Where I would find contacts

  • LinkedIn: current Heads / VPs of Cards, Loyalty, Digital Banking, CRM and Partnerships.
  • Bank annual reports, investor presentations and public product pages.
  • Card-network partner ecosystems and industry events.
  • Warm introductions through existing fintech, issuer, merchant and network relationships.
  • Razorpay internal referrals from Sales, Enterprise, Product and existing partner teams.
  • Targeted executive roundtables and small working sessions, not mass event badge collection.

Example first-touch message

“I noticed your team has been investing in the card and digital-banking proposition. I work on partnerships around transaction-led offers, loyalty and merchant-funded rewards. I’d like to understand the priorities for your card portfolio this year and explore whether a focused pilot could improve activation or spend without simply increasing blanket cashback costs. Would a short discovery conversation next week be useful?”

This is a sample outreach message, not a claim of an existing relationship. I would personalise the opening line with a verified account-specific trigger.

05 · Discovery and qualification

Diagnose the bank's problem before choosing the product.

Portfolio economics

  • Which card segment needs growth?
  • What are activation, active-card and spend-frequency baselines?
  • Where is the bank spending on cashback or rewards today?
  • What is the incremental contribution after incentives?

Customer experience

  • Where do customers discover and redeem offers?
  • Which segments are underserved?
  • What drives drop-off or low redemption?
  • Which channels can the bank activate?

Delivery feasibility

  • What systems and APIs are involved?
  • What data can be shared and under what consent?
  • What are security, residency and compliance constraints?
  • Who owns budget, sign-off and launch?

Qualification output: a one-page opportunity brief with problem statement, executive sponsor, business owner, target segment, baseline metric, desired outcome, budget path, integration complexity, decision process, competing priorities and next dated action.

06 · Build the commercial proposition

Translate the pain into a measurable bank business case.

I would prepare a tailored CVP deck and a simple model rather than send a generic product presentation. The proposal would show the current state, the target segment, the proposed Engage capability, the customer journey, funding responsibilities, delivery plan, risk controls and measurement framework.

Commercial levers to evaluate

  • Annual platform or programme fee, where the product and contract support it.
  • Implementation or integration fee for defined scope.
  • Campaign fees or usage-based pricing, where appropriate.
  • Per-active-user, per-transaction or issuance/redemption components, if supported by the product model.
  • Merchant-funded or co-funded incentive pools.
  • Minimum commitment, volume tiers and expansion pricing.

These are negotiation structures to test with Razorpay Finance and Product, not confirmed rate-card items.

What the model must prove

  • Incremental active cards and transactions, not vanity impressions.
  • Incremental spend versus a holdout or credible baseline.
  • Cost per incremental active customer.
  • Redemption, repeat usage and campaign contribution.
  • Funding split between bank, merchant and platform.
  • Payback, margin, delivery cost and downside sensitivity.

07 · Close the deal and facilitate execution

Own the commercial thread; make the handoffs deliberate.

1. Mutual action plan. Agree decision-makers, milestones, owners, documents, dependencies and target dates with the bank. Every meeting ends with a named owner and dated next step.
2. Solution and scope. Bring Product and Engineering into a working session. Confirm use case, supported capabilities, integration pattern, data requirements, reporting, volumes and non-functional needs.
3. Commercial negotiation. Work with Finance on pricing, contribution margin, minimum commitment, volume tiers, funding flows, payment terms, settlement and renewal mechanics.
4. Risk and contracting. Coordinate Legal, Compliance, InfoSec and bank procurement on data processing, consent, security, liability, SLAs, audit rights, termination and change control.
5. Launch governance. Run a weekly deal-to-live meeting across bank, Product, Engineering, Operations, Finance and Support. Track blockers, owners, due dates and escalation paths.
6. Pilot and scale. Launch to a defined cohort, compare results against baseline or holdout, fix journey friction, and agree scale-up gates before expanding to the full portfolio.
My role in the middle: I would not pretend to be the engineer, lawyer or reconciliation operator. I would make sure the right expert is involved, the decision is documented, the dependency has an owner, the bank receives one coherent answer, and the commercial objective survives the handoff.
08 · First 90 days

Build pipeline, create proof, then repeat what works.

Days 1–30

Learn & map

  • Master Engage product capabilities, exclusions and integration patterns.
  • Understand pricing guardrails, margin floors and legal/compliance requirements.
  • Map target markets and build 40–60 named accounts.
  • Score accounts by fit, urgency, access, value and implementation feasibility.
  • Build stakeholder maps and personalised outreach sequences.

Outputs: territory plan, account briefs, ICP, discovery guide and initial qualified pipeline.

Days 31–60

Open & qualify

  • Run structured outbound and warm-introduction campaigns.
  • Hold discovery meetings with business owners and executive sponsors.
  • Qualify budget, need, authority, timeline and integration readiness.
  • Run product workshops for priority accounts.
  • Develop bank-specific CVPs and pilot proposals.

Outputs: qualified opportunities, stakeholder coverage, solution workshops and proposals.

Days 61–90

Advance & close

  • Move priority deals through commercial and technical validation.
  • Negotiate scope, commercials and mutual action plans.
  • Start contracting and vendor due diligence early.
  • Get at least one opportunity to a defined pilot or contracting milestone, subject to sales-cycle reality.
  • Document objections and refine the pitch.

Outputs: late-stage pipeline, commercial proposals, signed or pilot-ready opportunities and repeatable playbooks.

10 · How I would expand after the first win

Land one use case, prove value, expand the relationship.

Within the same bank

  • Expand from one card segment to premium, co-brand, debit or broader retail portfolios.
  • Add new benefit categories, merchant partners or reward formats.
  • Move from one-off campaigns to always-on contextual engagement.
  • Extend from offers into loyalty wallets, gift cards or enterprise loyalty where the need fits.
  • Expand to another geography or business line after local validation.

Across the ecosystem

  • Use a successful issuer case to open peer-bank conversations, with permission and approved proof points.
  • Build joint propositions with card networks and processors.
  • Recruit merchant partners to co-fund relevant customer incentives.
  • Create repeatable vertical plays for travel, dining, ecommerce, fuel and lifestyle.
  • Develop referral and co-selling motions with ecosystem partners.

12 · Partner objections & rejection handling

Turn a “no” into useful information, not an argument.

A partner saying no is not automatically a dead lead, and it is not an invitation to keep pushing the same pitch. My job is to understand whether the objection is about value, timing, budget, trust, risk, capability or internal ownership. I would acknowledge the concern, ask one or two diagnostic questions, respond with evidence or a smaller next step, and agree a clear disposition in the CRM.

Objection-handling rule: never argue, overpromise, discount blindly or bypass the partner's governance. If the objection reveals a genuine mismatch, disqualify respectfully. If it reveals a solvable blocker, create an owner-led action plan.

My repeatable response method

Step 1 · Listen

Acknowledge without defending

Let the partner finish. Repeat the concern in neutral language to confirm I understood it correctly.

Step 2 · Diagnose

Find the real blocker

Ask whether the issue is need, value, budget, timing, authority, integration, compliance or trust. Avoid guessing.

Step 3 · Resolve or exit

Agree a practical next step

Offer evidence, a workshop, a revised scope or a future date. If there is no fit, close respectfully and record why.

How I would classify the outcome in the CRM

  • Active opportunity: confirmed problem, business owner, next meeting and a plausible decision path.
  • Blocked / nurture: genuine fit but timing, budget, procurement or technical capacity is not ready. Record the exact trigger and revisit date.
  • Closed-lost: no business case, no sponsor, incumbent locked in, poor economics or no feasible delivery path.
  • Disqualified: product/geography/compliance mismatch, unacceptable risk, or partner explicitly declines further contact.

I would review recurring objections monthly with Sales, Product and Partnerships. If several banks raise the same issue, it may signal a product gap, unclear positioning, pricing mismatch or weak proof point rather than a prospecting problem.

13 · Partner FAQ

Questions I would be ready to answer before asking for a commitment.

These are example partner questions and the response approach I would use. Product-specific commitments must be checked with the relevant Razorpay team before being shared externally.

What business problem does Razorpay Engage solve for a bank?

I would connect the proposition to the bank's stated objective: card activation, transaction frequency, spend, loyalty engagement, reward redemption or campaign effectiveness. I would first establish the baseline and target segment, then map only the relevant Engage capability to that outcome.

How is this different from our existing loyalty or cashback programme?

I would not claim it is automatically better. I would compare the current programme against the bank's priorities: customer relevance, merchant coverage, campaign speed, redemption experience, operational effort, measurement and total economics. The proposal should address a demonstrated gap or complement what already works.

Can you guarantee a specific increase in card spend or customer activation?

No responsible partner should guarantee an uplift without validated data and agreed assumptions. I would propose a measurable pilot with baseline metrics, a defined audience, a comparison method, an attribution window and pre-agreed success criteria.

How long will implementation take?

I would not quote a fixed timeline before technical discovery. Timing depends on integration pattern, data availability, security review, bank procurement, product scope and internal resources. After discovery, I would provide a dependency-based plan with owners and milestone dates.

Will we need to share personally identifiable or transaction-level data?

I would not assume that data is required or request it informally. Product, Legal, Compliance and InfoSec teams must establish the minimum data needed, purpose, consent or other lawful basis, access controls, retention, residency and contractual safeguards before any exchange.

Can the bank control which customers receive an offer?

I would document the bank's segmentation, eligibility, exclusions, consent and campaign approval requirements, then validate how those requirements are supported by the product. I would never promise a targeting or suppression capability without product confirmation.

Who funds the customer incentive?

That depends on the commercial design and participating parties. I would model bank-funded, merchant-funded or co-funded options only where feasible, and clarify caps, settlement, reconciliation, liability and campaign economics before launch.

What happens if a campaign underperforms?

We would review the agreed metrics and funnel: eligible audience, reach, activation, click-through where relevant, redemption, incremental transactions, repeat behaviour and cost. We would identify whether the issue is targeting, offer attractiveness, channel placement, customer journey or merchant coverage, then agree whether to optimise, pause or stop.

Can we start with a pilot instead of a large annual commitment?

Where product, commercial and operational teams support it, I would propose a tightly scoped pilot with a defined segment, duration, budget, responsibilities and success criteria. A pilot should have a decision path for scale-up, not become an indefinite free proof of concept.

How will we measure ROI?

We would agree the baseline, eligible population, campaign and comparison periods, attribution rules, incentive costs and incremental contribution. I would distinguish total campaign activity from genuinely incremental outcomes and ask Finance to validate the calculation.

What support will our team receive after signing?

I would define a named relationship owner, implementation owner, escalation route, operating cadence, reporting expectations and service responsibilities in the launch plan and contract. The exact support model and SLAs must be confirmed with the delivery team.

What if our procurement, risk or legal team rejects the proposal?

I would ask for the specific control or clause that is not acceptable, bring the appropriate internal specialist into the discussion, and assess whether the issue can be resolved through clarification or a revised scope. If the requested exception creates unacceptable risk, I would not bypass the control.
